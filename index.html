The story that runs through every topic

Twenty-one unrelated examples produce twenty-one shallow memories. One company examined twenty-one ways produces a system you can reason about — and defend under questioning.

Every Expanded guide in this library now carries a Prairie Harvest story thread: the same fictional grain co-operative, the same people, the same systems, and one shared incident examined from that topic's own chair. Read the canon once before you start, and return to it whenever a page mentions a name you do not recognise.

Prairie Harvest Co-operative — the story canon

A farmer-owned grain co-operative in Iowa, midway through a three-year transformation. 4,200 grower-members, 11 elevators, $1.4B revenue, an ECC-to-S/4HANA programme, a portal being rebuilt, and one very expensive September.

The incident every topic examines

During peak harvest a fibre cut takes one elevator offline for three hours. The scale house keeps weighing and queues deliveries locally — exactly as designed. A mid-outage PC restart resets the local sequence counter, so on reconnect the replay's deduplication key is no longer unique. The duplicate check reads a replica fifteen minutes behind and sees nothing. 402 duplicate scale tickets are accepted, crediting roughly 358,000 bushels of grain that never existed. Contracts push past tolerance, genuine grain reprices at spot, and Thursday's settlement run pays 340 growers on quantities that were never delivered — about $1.8M.

It surfaces eleven days later when a farmer phones to say his statement shows 94 loads and he delivered 90.

Why this one incident teaches everything

It crosses every layer

Offline client, message queue, integration control, ERP business rule, database consistency, monitoring threshold, and human process. Each is a different day in this library.

Nobody was stupid

Offline-first was right for a scale house. Reading a replica is standard. Widening alerts for harvest was reasonable. Three good decisions, unsafe only in combination.

It has a number

$1.8M and eleven days give every improvement you propose a denominator — and every claim you make an arithmetic check.

It ends with a person

Not a dashboard. A farmer with a notebook. That is both memorable and true to how these things are actually found.

The three-level rule

Every story block describes the same true events at three levels — ground, practitioner, leader. What changes is the precision of the language, never the facts. Nothing taught at beginner level has to be un-learned later, and there are no toy analogies to outgrow.

The honesty boundary

Prairie Harvest does not exist. It is a teaching scaffold, and it is deliberately realistic — which makes it easy to narrate as though you lived it. Never put it on a CV or offer it as your own experience.

Use it to explain how you think. Use your twenty years to explain what you have done. Read the full boundary, and see Readiness-1 for the exercise that converts the story's vocabulary into your own real examples.

Orientation

This is a learning curriculum, not a reading list

Use the Expanded files to learn and build, then use the paired Quick Reviews for retrieval and revision. Each study day combines reading, recall, an artifact, and spoken practice. The schedule intentionally gives the largest Expanded guides multiple days.

1 · UnderstandRead diagrams and beginner layer
2 · ReconstructExplain without looking
3 · ApplyBuild or analyse an artifact
4 · DefendAnswer follow-ups aloud

Standard weekday

90 minutes learning, 45 minutes notes/diagram reconstruction, 45 minutes practice or implementation. Total about 3 hours.

Deep-work day

3–4 hours for SAP, Selenium, Playwright, API, or AI solution labs. Stop at a natural section boundary rather than skimming.

Weekly review

Retrieve concepts before rereading. Rebuild one diagram, run one practical, answer ten questions, and repair the weakest area.

Story bank

For every topic, store a truthful situation, your decision/action, scale, result, trade-off, and learning. Never invent experience.

If a discussion is close: use the relevant Quick Reviews first, then open the corresponding Expanded sections for concepts you cannot explain confidently.

The rule that makes this work

Week 8 is reserved for practice, not reading. The most common failure in a compressed preparation is arriving at the discussion having consumed a great deal and rehearsed none of it. Recognition is not retrieval — being able to follow an explanation is not the same as producing it under pressure with someone pushing back.

If you fall behind, cut from the role-dependent tier. Do not cut week 8.
